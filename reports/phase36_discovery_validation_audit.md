# Phase 36 — Discovery vs. Validation Process Audit

**Audits the research architecture itself (Phases 27, 29-35), not any specific candidate. No new backtest run.**

---

## How hypotheses have been generated

Every hypothesis across Phases 30/33/35 traces to an explicit, written economic rationale recorded *before* backtesting (`reports/non_jpy_candidate_registry.csv`, `phase33_candidate_registry.csv`, `phase35_candidate_registry.csv`) — none were generated by scanning results and reverse-engineering a story. Sources of hypotheses have consistently been: (a) this project's own prior findings (e.g. H4/H5 explicitly motivated by Phase 34's diagnosis of Phase 33's failures; USDCAD momentum in Phase 33 extending the project's own CADJPY cross-sectional momentum finding), and (b) an explicit research backlog already written into `PROJECT_REPORT.md` before any of Phases 29-35 began (e.g. the XAUUSD London/NY hypothesis). **This is a healthy generation process** — hypotheses are traceable to evidence or prior findings, not invented ad hoc.

## How many hypotheses are generated before testing, and how many are tested

Every hypothesis that was generated (recorded in a registry) was also tested — there is no evidence of a larger, unrecorded candidate pool that was silently pre-filtered before registration. Phase 30 generated and tested 60 cells (one mechanism family, swept across a small paired grid); Phase 33 and Phase 35 generated and tested exactly 2 and 5 hypotheses respectively, matching their own explicit "deliberately constrained, no grid search" instructions. **No hypothesis has ever been dropped from a registry after being registered but before being tested.**

## Is OOS being used for discovery?

**No, and this is independently verifiable from git history.** Both Phase 33's and Phase 35's pre-registrations were committed in a separate commit *before* any candidate backtest ran (`8bcd30e` and `7821cd7` respectively), and `src/phase34_research_audit.py` and this phase's own checks confirm each preregistration file has exactly one commit touching it — never edited after results existed. The OOS window itself (2025-05-01 to 2026-08-14) has been inspected exactly once per hypothesis in each of Phase 33 and Phase 35; no hypothesis has been re-parameterized and re-tested against the same OOS window after an initial failure.

## Are candidates repeatedly modified?

**No.** Every rejected candidate in the ledger (`reports/phase36_research_ledger.csv`) was tested exactly once at its pre-registered parameter set (plus the pre-registered ±20% sensitivity check, which is a diagnostic, not a search). Phase 34/35's H4 and H5 are **new, independently pre-registered hypotheses** motivated by the *diagnosis* of Phase 33's H4/H5-equivalent failures — not modifications of the same registered candidate re-run with adjusted parameters. This distinction is real and load-bearing: a modification would re-use the same registration and re-test after seeing a result; these are fresh registrations, committed separately, before their own new results existed.

## Are failed candidates being rescued?

**No instance found across the ledger.** Every rejected candidate's final classification traces to a mechanically-applied, pre-registered rule (e.g. Phase 33/35's classification order, applied "regardless of which candidate they favor or disfavor" per their own preregistration text) — no rejected candidate was subsequently reclassified upward after additional favorable evidence was found. AUDUSD Monday LONG (the one candidate that reached PROMISING) has been re-evaluated against each new phase's framework multiple times (Phases 32, 34, 35) **without ever being promoted or having its own evidence altered** — each re-evaluation applied the *current* framework to the *same, frozen* prior evidence, which is the correct behavior, not a rescue.

## Is the same data being repeatedly reused?

**Yes, and this is a real, disclosed limitation, not a hidden one.** Every candidate across Phase 30/33/35 was tested against the same underlying MT5 MetaQuotes-Demo price history for its 2023-2026 window. Phase 34 and this phase both flag this explicitly: the OOS window (2025-05-01 to 2026-08-14) is not independent across hypotheses that share an instrument or overlapping time period — e.g. H1 and H4 both use USDCAD's H1/H4/D1 series over the identical calendar window. **This means the project's cumulative "8 of 16 families tested" tally should not be read as 8 fully independent draws from market history** — they share substantial calendar overlap, which is directly relevant to RQ4.

## Is multiple testing adequately tracked?

**Yes, consistently since Phase 30.** Every phase from 30 onward has produced an explicit registry, a pre-declared screening/classification bar, and a disclosure of every tested cell/hypothesis regardless of outcome — this phase's own consolidated ledger (`reports/phase36_research_ledger.csv`, 68 rows) was built entirely from these pre-existing disclosures, confirming none were hidden. See `reports/phase36_multiple_testing_audit.csv` for the cumulative counts.

---

## Answer: "Is our current process capable of finding an edge without excessive multiple-testing bias?"

**Yes, structurally** — the pre-registration discipline (frozen rules, separate commits, mechanical classification, full disclosure of failures) is sound and has been consistently applied since Phase 30, strengthening with each phase (Phase 33 added the OOS-sub-half and parameter-perturbation checks; Phase 34 added sample-size preconditions; Phase 35 kept them). **The process is not what has produced the null results — a genuinely disciplined search has, so far, not found a qualifying candidate.** The one real caveat (§"same data reused") is about the *independence* of evidence across hypotheses sharing a calendar window and instrument, not about discovery-vs-validation contamination — those are different concerns, and only the latter has been rigorously avoided by design.
