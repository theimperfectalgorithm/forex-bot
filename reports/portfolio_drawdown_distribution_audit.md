# Portfolio Drawdown Distribution Audit

**Purpose:** answer exactly one question — *"Is the current prop-account
losing period statistically normal for the 8-strategy portfolio we
designed?"* — using only existing historical trade-level data. **No
strategy was modified, no parameter optimized, no new research
conducted.** This is a benchmark, not a profitability forecast.

**Experiments:** EXP-092 through EXP-095, `experiments/experiments.csv`.
**Script:** `src/phase25_portfolio_drawdown_audit.py`. **Full log:** `reports/phase25_audit_log.txt`. **Data:** `data/phase20_trades.csv` (source), `data/phase25_portfolio_trades.csv`, `data/phase25_drawdown_episodes.csv`.

## Data-quality correction (found and fixed before use, documented transparently)

Phase 20's reconstruction (`src/phase20_volatility_regime_diagnostics.py`)
used `risk_pct=0.005` (0.50%) for **all three** ARB strategies, including
XAUUSD ARB — but XAUUSD ARB's frozen live weight is **0.25%**, not
0.50% (GBPJPY ARB and CADJPY ARB are correctly 0.50%). Since dollar P&L
scales linearly with risk_pct (R-multiples do not), XAUUSD ARB's dollar
P&L was rescaled ×0.5 before building the portfolio equity curve. This
is a correction to a data artifact from an earlier phase, not a new
optimization or parameter change — it makes the reconstruction match
the frozen weights you specified.

## Portfolio-simulation simplification (stated, not hidden)

Each strategy's dollar P&L was originally generated by its own
independent $100,000-balance compounding simulation. This audit sums
those 8 independently-compounding streams onto **one shared $100,000
reference capital** to build a combined portfolio equity curve — the
same simplification this project's own earlier portfolio-model work
used. It does **not** model cross-strategy compounding interaction
(e.g. one strategy's drawdown temporarily shrinking the capital base
available to the others). This is a known limitation of the benchmark,
not an error.

## Part 3 — Monte Carlo (10,000 trade-order-shuffle simulations)

**Methodology:** the actual historical trade set (same 3,160 trades,
same individual P&L values, same eventual total profit — order-invariant
by construction) is shuffled into 10,000 random arrival orders. For each
shuffled order, the equity curve, max drawdown, and max losing streak
are recomputed. This produces a distribution of **path-dependent**
outcomes consistent with the historical trade population. **It does not
prove future profitability — it only characterizes how much drawdown/
streak variation this specific set of historical trades could plausibly
produce under a different arrival order.**

| Percentile | Max drawdown (% depth) | Max losing streak (trades) |
|---|---|---|
| 50th | 5.05% | 7.0 |
| 75th | 5.99% | 8.0 |
| 90th | 7.14% | 9.0 |
| 95th | 7.93% | 10.0 |
| 99th | 9.96% | 11.0 |

**Two things worth flagging directly:**

1. **The actual historical (real chronological order) max drawdown of
   -10.51% sits at the 99.4th percentile of the 10,000-run Monte Carlo
   distribution** — i.e., the real order in which trades actually
   happened produced a *worse* drawdown than 99.4% of random reshuffles
   of the same trades. This is meaningful: it means the real-world
   sequencing of losses was not typical random luck — the actual
   historical losses clustered together more than chance alone would
   predict (consistent with the JPY-correlation finding in Part 6).
2. **The actual historical max losing streak of 14 trades sits at the
   100.0th percentile** of the Monte Carlo distribution — worse than
   every one of the 10,000 shuffles. The real portfolio has, in its own
   real history, already produced a losing streak more extreme than
   random reordering of its own trades would typically generate.

## Part 4 — Current prop account comparison

Given: prop drawdown ≈ -3.4% to -3.8%, prop losing streak ≈ 9 trades.

| Metric | Value | Percentile of MC distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Prop drawdown, low estimate (-3.4%) | | **2.7th** |
| Prop drawdown, high estimate (-3.8%) | | **9.3th** |
| Prop losing streak (9 trades) | | **93.9th** |

**1. Is the current prop drawdown unusual?** No. At -3.4% to -3.8%, it
sits at only the 2.7th–9.3rd percentile of the Monte Carlo drawdown
distribution — meaning 90-97% of random-order simulations of this exact
portfolio's own historical trades produced a *deeper* drawdown than
what the prop account is currently showing. The current dollar drawdown
is **below the median (5.05%)** of what this portfolio has historically
been capable of producing purely from trade-sequence variance.

**2. Is the current 9-trade losing streak unusual?** Yes, on a relative
basis — it sits at the 93.9th percentile, meaning only about 6% of
random-order simulations produced a streak this long or longer. But it
is **not unprecedented**: the real historical portfolio has already
produced a 14-trade streak under actual chronological conditions, and
the MC 99th percentile (11 trades) is not far above the current 9.

**3. What percentile does it resemble?** Drawdown: mild, ~3rd–9th
percentile (well below median). Losing streak: elevated, ~94th
percentile (in the upper tail, but below the historical maximum).

**4. Is it inside the expected range?** Yes for drawdown (comfortably).
For the losing streak, it is inside the range the portfolio has
actually produced before (up to 14), though in the upper portion of
that range.

**5. Is it approaching a statistically concerning region?** Not on the
drawdown dimension. On the losing-streak dimension, it is in the upper
tail (90th-95th percentile band) but has not crossed the 99th
percentile (11 trades) or the portfolio's own historical maximum (14
trades) — it is elevated, not yet in the most extreme region this
portfolio has demonstrably experienced.

## Part 7 — Final classification

# **B. ELEVATED BUT WITHIN EXPECTED RANGE**

**Reasoning:**

- The current prop **dollar drawdown (-3.4% to -3.8%) is not unusual**
  — it sits well below the median of what this exact portfolio's own
  historical trade sequence is capable of producing from ordinary
  path-dependent variance (MC median 5.05%, current sits at the 3rd-9th
  percentile). This is the primary risk metric, and it shows nothing
  concerning.
- The current **9-trade losing streak is elevated** (93.9th percentile
  of the Monte Carlo streak distribution) but **is not unprecedented**
  — the portfolio's own real historical trade sequence has already
  produced a longer streak (14 trades) than the current one, and the
  Monte Carlo 99th percentile (11 trades) has not been reached.
- This combination — mild dollar drawdown, elevated-but-not-extreme
  losing streak — is a real and internally consistent pattern for this
  portfolio, not a contradiction: with 87-88% of the portfolio in
  correlated JPY exposure and most individual strategies carrying only
  0.25%-0.50% risk per trade, a run of several small, correlated JPY
  losses can produce a longer-than-typical streak count without a large
  dollar drawdown, exactly because the individual trade risk is small.
- **This classification does not rule out C or D becoming appropriate
  if the streak continues or the drawdown deepens** — at 9 trades the
  streak is already past the 90th percentile; a few more losses would
  put it in genuinely rare territory (past the 14-trade historical
  maximum, or past the 99th Monte Carlo percentile). This audit is a
  benchmark against the past, not a prediction of what happens next.
- **No strategy-level attribution for the actual current period is
  available** (Part 5) — this classification is based solely on the
  portfolio-level drawdown/streak benchmark (Parts 2-4), which IS
  answerable from the summary statistics provided. Whether the current
  streak is being driven by ARB, AMR, or general variance cannot be
  determined without the real prop trade log.
